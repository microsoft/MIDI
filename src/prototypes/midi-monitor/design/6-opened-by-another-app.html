When a window opens with an endpoint on the command lineWhat happens
ModeOpens in the mode you used last. Simple mode picks that endpoint (and the group and channel, when they're given), the way it does today. Advanced mode checks that endpoint's groups and nothing else. Two new switches, --simple and --advanced, let a tool ask for one, without changing the mode you'll get next time.
Your saved sourcesSet aside, not changed. Checking and unchecking in this window only affects this window. Add to my sources adds what's checked here to your saved list. Switch to my sources swaps this window over to your saved list, and from then on it saves like any other window.
Filters, columns, zoom, order, themeShared, and saved as usual. They're about how you like to read messages, not about what you're watching, so a tool opening the Monitor has no reason to override them.
Switching mode in this windowSaved as usual: it's something you did, not something the tool did.
Several windows open at onceEach one keeps its own sources while it's open. A window that is using your saved sources saves them when they change, so the last change made wins, the way the column layout already works.
Starting without a command lineOpens in the last mode, with your saved sources (advanced) or the device, group and channel you watched last (simple). Simple mode doesn't remember the device today; it will (Pete, Nov 8), under the same rule: a command-line launch doesn't overwrite it.
  1. An info bar says why the sources look different. Without it, someone who opened MIDI Settings, clicked Monitor, and then saw their carefully chosen list replaced would assume it was lost. It stays until you close it or click one of its buttons.
  2. The two ways out. Keep what the tool asked for alongside your usual list, or go back to your usual list. Doing nothing is the third way, and it's safe: close the window and your saved list is still there next time.
  3. Filters still apply. Your saved filter (here, Clock and Active Sensing hidden) carries over, since it's about how you read messages. The chips show it, so nothing is hidden without saying so.
