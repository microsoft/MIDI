  1. Draw or record. Click to add a note, drag to set its length, or record from the track's source. Softer notes are drawn fainter.
  2. Per-note pitch bend. Each note carries its own bend, drawn as a line inside the note: a vibrato on B3, a fall on A3 and a scoop into D4. Edit it there or in the Bend lane.
  3. Exact pitch. G3 +50¢ uses the MIDI 2.0 Pitch 7.9 note attribute, so one note can be a quarter tone sharp without retuning the whole channel.
  4. Values at full size. Velocity is 16-bit here. Show values as switches every number in the window to 0 to 127, or to percent.
  5. Per-note controllers. The Pan lane is a registered per-note controller, so the long E4 moves on its own while other notes stay put.
  6. Writing parts that fit. Ghost notes from Pad chords and the E minor shading are guides only. Nothing about them is sent.
  7. No silent losses. The bar at the top says what this destination does with all of this, and the inspector says what a MIDI 1.0 device would get instead.
  8. Chance. Any note can have a chance below 100%, the same idea as a pattern step. That makes this clip play differently each time through, which export has to deal with (see comp 7).
