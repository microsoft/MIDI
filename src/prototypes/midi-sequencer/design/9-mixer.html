Close up of one strip, twice the size.
  1. Expression, reverb and chorus are small knobs. Drag up or down, turn the mouse wheel, or use the arrow keys. Double-click puts a knob back where General MIDI synths start. The value shows while you move it.
  2. Pan is a knob that lights up from the center, and says how far left or right: L24, C or R18. Double-click centers it. Screen readers hear "left 24", "center" and "right 18".
  3. The volume fader has a cap you drag, on a scale from 0 to 127. The longer mark is 100, where General MIDI synths start.
  4. MIDI meter (proposed). How hard the track's notes play, as they're sent. It isn't sound: the sequencer never hears the device. It gives the strip life while the music plays, the way the MIDI meters on a DAW's MIDI tracks do.
  5. The volume as a number, in a small display. A dot beside it means the control follows the track's automation lane.
  6. M, S and W mute and solo the track, and write your moves into automation while it plays. Lit in the same colors as the track list.
  7. The track's name on its color, with where it plays underneath, at the bottom of the strip like on a mixing desk.
  8. The selected track is outlined, the same track that's selected in the track list.
Unchanged: what each control sends (controllers 7, 10, 11, 91 and 93), what's kept when you move one, the W button, automation lanes, and the hint at the top. Not shown: what a strip looks like when the panel is short. The fader gets shorter first, then the reverb and chorus knobs fold into one row with expression hidden behind a "more" button.
