The timeline's scroll bar, and zoom

The timeline gets a scroll bar of its own, drawn by the app like the rest of the timeline. It zooms as well as scrolls, and you can switch it between two kinds: a plain bar, and an overview of the whole sequence like Bitwig Studio's, with a line for each track and the sections by name. It goes under the timeline or above the ruler, as you choose. Below are comps of both kinds in Tactile and Aluminum, in their light looks and, dark, in your aqua. The timelines in the comps are drawn at the zoom they show: 12 bars across, the whole sequence, and 3½ bars.

The comps are pages over the main window comp (make.ps1 builds them; scroll.js and scroll.css add the bar). Click any picture for its page. The research on what other DAWs do is near the end.

What it does, either kind

  • Scroll in time: drag the view (the plain bar's thumb, or the overview's frame). Click outside it to jump there. Shift + wheel, a tilting wheel, or a two-finger sideways swipe on a touchpad scrolls in time; the wheel on its own still scrolls the tracks.
  • Zoom: drag either end of the view, which keeps the other end where it is. Ctrl + wheel zooms about the pointer. A pinch on a touch screen or a touchpad zooms time only, about the pinch. The zoom controls at the bar's end zoom about the playhead when it's in view, and about the middle when it isn't. Drag the view up or down to zoom, as Live's overview does. Double-click the view to zoom to fit the sequence, and double-click again to go back.
  • Keys (when the timeline has focus): Ctrl+= zoom in, Ctrl+- zoom out, Ctrl+0 zoom to fit, Ctrl+Shift+0 zoom to the selection. Bitwig and Resolve use the same; see the research for the others.
  • The grid follows the zoom. Far out it marks bars, every fourth one stronger, and the ruler numbers every other bar. In the middle it marks bars and beats. Close in it adds sixteenths, and the ruler numbers the beats. Lines are never closer than about 6 pixels, and numbers never closer than about 40.
  • Limits: out to the whole sequence and a little room after it; in to about one beat across the timeline.
  • The switch at the bar's left end changes between the overview and the plain bar. Right-click the bar for the same choice, for where the bar goes, and to zoom to fit or to the selection. The same two settings are in the app's settings.

The overview, under the timeline

A line for each track where its clips are, in its color. A closed folder is one line. Muted clips are faint. The sections (the tempo track's tags) are named across the top, with a mark where each starts. You also see the loop and the playhead, and, under the lines, where the selected clip's linked copies play, the way Visual Studio's scroll bar marks what a search found. What's outside the view is veiled. The view is a frame: drag it to move, or drag a grip at either end to zoom.

The plain bar

The same view and zoom with nothing else in the bar but the playhead's mark. The tooltip shows what pointing at an end tells you.

Above the ruler

With the setting at the top, the overview lines up with the ruler and the timeline under it, and the zoom moves into the track list's toolbar, beside the switch, as Logic Pro puts its zoom above the tracks.

What it means here

  • Ctrl + wheel zooms, about the pointer, as in Cubase, Live and FL Studio. Pro Tools, Premiere and Resolve use Alt instead, so the modifier could become a setting later.
  • A touchpad's pinch comes free with it. For an app that doesn't use DirectManipulation, Windows sends a precision touchpad's pinch as the wheel with Ctrl held (WM_MOUSEWHEEL with MK_CONTROL) [19], so in WinUI it arrives as PointerWheelChanged with the Control modifier. One handler covers both.
  • A touch screen's pinch comes through manipulation events (ManipulationModes Scale, ManipulationDelta) [20], which come from touch only; a touchpad's pinch never produces them (community) [21]. InteractionTracker may pick up the touchpad too; that needs a try on real hardware.
  • Shift + wheel scrolls in time, as in Cubase, Studio One and REAPER.
  • Dragging the thumb's ends is how Premiere and Resolve zoom, and something Cubase users have asked for.
  • Overviews are at the top in Live, Pro Tools, Resolve and Cubase's overview line, and at the bottom in Bitwig, where it replaces the scroll bar as it does here. Both are familiar, which is a good reason for the setting. From Live, two gestures worth copying: drag the view up or down to zoom, and double-click to fit.
  • What a zoom stays about differs: the pointer in Logic, Live and Cubase, and the edit selection in Pro Tools. Here, the pointer for the wheel and a pinch, and for keys and buttons the playhead when it's in view, else the middle, much as Logic does.

Building it

  • The timeline becomes one control, TimelineView, which owns the time axis (where the view starts, in ticks, and how many ticks a pixel is) and the vertical scroll. It draws the ruler, the lanes and the playhead with Win2D as now, keeps the pinned rows apart from the ones that scroll, and hosts the bar. The track headers and the launcher keep their own controls and follow its vertical scroll.
  • The bar is a control of its own, TimelineScrollBar, drawn with Win2D through the theme engine. Each style draws its well, view, grips, veil and zoom controls from parts in its theme file (timeline.scrollbar.*, timeline.overview.*), the way the clips and launcher pads are drawn now, so Night drive, Tactile, Aluminum and Vellum each get their own without code of their own. The piano roll and the drum pattern editor can use the plain bar later for their own zoom.
  • Zoom keeps the point you zoom about still: the tick under the pointer (or the pinch, or the end you didn't drag) stays where it is on screen. Steps from the keys and the controls glide over about 120 ms; wheels, pinches and drags follow your hand without easing.
  • The overview is drawn once into an image when the sequence changes, and the view, the veil and the playhead are drawn over it as they move, so it costs nothing while playing.
  • Input: pointer capture for dragging the view and its ends. PointerWheelChanged for the wheel: Ctrl zooms, which also takes a touchpad's pinch, since Windows sends it as Ctrl + wheel; Shift or a horizontal wheel scrolls. Manipulation events (ManipulationModes TranslateX and Scale) for a touch screen's pinch and drag.
  • Accessibility and keys: the bar takes focus. The arrow keys move the view, and Ctrl with an arrow zooms. UI Automation exposes it as a scroll bar whose value is the view's start and whose view size is the zoom. Narrator reads, for example, "Bars 5 to 17 of 26".
  • Settings: TimelineScrollBar (overview or plain) and TimelineScrollBarPlace (bottom or top), each with the bar's menu and the app's settings to change it. The zoom and the view are saved with the sequence, as they are now.

What I'd suggest

The overview under the timeline by default, with the plain bar a click away. Both kinds zoom by dragging an end, with Ctrl + wheel and with a pinch. Put keys at the bar's end (zoom out, zoom in, fit), the easiest to find and to touch; the slider is a close second if you'd rather see where the zoom is. Offer the top placement through the setting, for people who know an overview there from Live, Pro Tools or Cubase, with the zoom moving to the track list's toolbar.
