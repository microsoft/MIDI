3. Pattern clips: the step sequencer. A pattern is a clip like any other: it sits in a launcher slot or on the timeline, loops, and plays to the track's destination. Inside, it's a grid of steps. Each lane has its own length, so a 12-step hat against a 16-step kick drifts and comes back, and each step can have a chance, a ratchet, a condition, a nudge and controller locks.

  1. A pattern is a clip. It launches, loops, links and sits on the timeline like a notes clip, so a step sequencer needs no transport, clock or routing of its own.
  2. Lanes keep their own length. The hi-hat repeats every 12 steps and the side stick every 7, against a 16-step kick. The status bar says when everything lines up again.
  3. Per-step chance, ratchet, condition and nudge. The snare ghost plays 35% of the time, the hat on step 7 plays three quick hits, the open hat plays every second pass, and both claps are nudged a little early.
  4. Locks. A step can send a controller value for that step only. On a MIDI 2.0 destination a lock can be a per-note controller, so it changes just that hit.
  5. The same result every time. Chance and conditions are worked out from a seed saved in the project, so playing from the start, jumping back, or exporting all give the same hits. Vary picks a new seed.
  6. Make notes turns the pattern into an ordinary notes clip for detailed editing. The pattern itself is left alone.
