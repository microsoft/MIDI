Aluminum, further

Aluminum in a dark version that sits with a dark mode desktop, and both versions in the window color you pick in Appearance. Your Sequencer's color is the dark aqua #031E24, and the plum, #33192F, is the one you picked for MIDI Clock. The original Aluminum is unchanged; the new looks are layers over it (aluminum-dark.css and aluminum-tint.css).

How the window color comes in, the way anodizing colors metal:

  • Your color brings the hue, and the theme brings the lightness. The app keeps one color for light and dark, so each surface keeps the depth it has in the theme and takes only the color's hue and some of its chroma. A dark color, a light one, a vivid one, and a quiet one all work in both themes.
  • The strips are anodized deep in it: the title bar, transport, and status bar, the chassis of the window. In dark, that's about the color as you picked it.
  • The plates take a whisper of it, capped so a vivid color doesn't flood them.
  • The readouts are backlit in it, and the LEDs that mean "on", the selection, the loop, and toggled buttons light up in it.
  • What stays as it is: what you made (track colors, clips, and notes), the keys and pads, which are objects on the panel, and the colors that mean something: play, record, mute, and solo.

To try another color, open any of the new pages with ?tint=RRGGBB, for example aluminum-dark.html?tint=1E2A44. Add &anodize=0 to keep the metal plain, so only the strips, readouts, and lights take the color: aqua, plain metal.
