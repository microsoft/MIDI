Porcelain, dark, and the window color

The approved second pass of Porcelain (no well, with the timeline on a field a step darker, the piano roll's color), in a dark version that sits with a dark mode desktop, and both versions in the window color you pick in Appearance: your Sequencer's dark aqua #031E24, and the plum, #33192F, you picked for MIDI Clock. The light second pass is as you approved it, apart from the darker field; dark and the window color are layers over it (porcelain-dark.css and porcelain-tint.css).

Aluminum anodizes its strips deep. Porcelain is one material, so the color glazes all of it instead:

  • Your color brings the hue, and the theme brings the lightness, as with Aluminum, so one color works in light and dark.
  • The glaze covers the whole body: the surface, the keys (the piano keys too), and the clip faces, which are pressed out of the same porcelain. Because it covers everything, it stays low, so it reads as a tint in the glaze rather than paint.
  • It pools in the hollows, as glaze does: the wells, the readouts, the timeline's field, and the piano roll take a little more color.
  • The shadows are a deep shade of it, and the light from the top left a pale tint of it. They're the soft ones you picked (Softer shadows), gentler again in light.
  • The LEDs on the keys, the selection, the loop, and toggled keys light up in it, and the readouts' digits are printed in it: a deep ink in light, a pale one in dark.
  • What stays as it is: the track caps, the notes, and the colors that mean something: play, record, mute, and solo.

To try another color, open any of these pages with ?tint=RRGGBB, for example porcelain-2-dark.html?tint=1E2A44. Add &glaze=0 for plain porcelain, so only the lights and readouts take the color: aqua, plain porcelain.
