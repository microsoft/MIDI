  1. Pinned rows. Tempo and meter, and Drums, are pinned. They stay at the top of the track list, the launcher and the timeline while everything under the shaded edge scrolls, like frozen rows in Excel.
  2. Folders. Synths is open and Keys is closed. A closed folder shows an outline of what its tracks play. M and S on a folder apply to every track inside it.
  3. Launcher and timeline share rows. A track follows the timeline until you launch a clip. Pad is playing a launched clip, so its timeline is covered, and Back to timeline returns it. Pad swell waits for bar 12.
  4. Three kinds of clip. Notes (Bass, Pad, Lead), pattern (Drums) and generator (Arp, Texture). Generator clips have a dashed edge because what they play is decided as they play.
  5. Linked clips. Bass line A is one clip placed four times. Edit it once and every copy changes. Make a copy to break the link.
  6. Automation on the track. SVF cutoff is drawn right under Bass. The Moog One is a MIDI 1.0 synth, so it gets NRPN 7:54 at 14 bits. A MIDI 2.0 device would get the same lane as a 32-bit assignable controller.
  7. Recording. Lead is armed (R) and recording into an empty slot in scene 3. What you play is echoed to its destination so you hear it. Capture turns what you just played into a clip, even if you never pressed record.
  8. The clip editor. The selected clip opens at the bottom, or in its own window. Values show at MIDI 2.0 resolution, with the MIDI 1.0 value next to them when it matters.
  9. Tags. Text at a moment. Intro, Groove, Break, Drop and Outro on the Tempo and meter track are about the whole sequence. Filter opens here (Bass) and Retake this (Lead) belong to one track. Tags are bookmarks, they export as markers, and they never send anything.
  10. Metronome and MIDI Keyboard. The metronome button sits next to Loop, and its settings are in comp 6. MIDI Keyboard in the editor opens Windows MIDI Keyboard already set to this clip's track: Moog One, channel 2.
