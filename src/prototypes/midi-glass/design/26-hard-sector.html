Screen 26 — Hard Sector, Soft Sector's machine in charcoal: the black desktops and keyboards that followed the beige ones, IBM's among them. Gray keys with white legends, black function keys, white print, and the same six LEDs in the same holders and windows. Named for the other kind of floppy disk. Same geometry and the same seven asks; only the colors change, and a script proves it by comparing the two stylesheets with every color taken out. What the dark changes is which cue does each job: the keys stand off the case by value rather than by shadow, a window is gloss rather than darkness, and an LED would now read even bare.

What the dark changes. The geometry is Soft Sector's to the pixel; what moves is which cue does each job. The keys stand off the case by value. A gray key is 2.26 : 1 from the charcoal where Soft Sector's light key was 1.31 : 1 from the putty, so the shadow that carried Soft Sector is only support here. A window is gloss, not darkness: black acrylic in a charcoal case is 1.21 : 1, so the reflection across it and the lit lip under it are what make it a window. An LED would read bare, at 4.55 to 11.38 : 1, and it keeps its holder anyway, because the geometry is shared. What does not change is the glow: a green halo on a gray key is 1.69 : 1, so a latched key still says "on" without a glow or color vision. The one tight number is the gray itself: white print needs the key darker than about #666669 and a slider cap needs it lighter than about #5D5D60.

Built — the same properties as Soft Sector, at other values

Nothing new. Every property Soft Sector names, Hard Sector names too: the grain as specks (GrainPercent, GrainStreak 1) and a black shadow (ShadowColor); the white print for every name, value, scale and pointer (InkColor, ValueColor, PointerColor, CapLineColor); gray slider caps (ThumbColor); the black key on the neutral slot (NeutralCaps), which lights the first slot's LED; on as a lamp and no fill (FillWhenOnPercent 0, LampShape dot); the glossy faceplate (InsetPanelColor); and the same six LEDs and MeterSlots. This is the Terminal Green argument again: once Soft Sector's asks are built, its dark twin is data.

Needs — Soft Sector's seven, and no more

The keycap, key travel and the smoked windows are still the theme. The dark raises one of them: a black window is only 1.21 : 1 from the charcoal, so the gloss named in the window ask is not a finish here, it is the whole of what makes a display a window. And the keycap's skirt must be able to be lighter at its back edge than its top, which is how a gray key catches the little light a black case has.

What it costs

Color is still an LED, and the same red, amber and green: the same 9.1 and 4.2 apart under simulated deuteranopia and protanopia, and the same answer, that on is also a position. What the dark adds: the gray key is a narrow band, about nine levels between white print that reads (4.96 : 1) and a slider cap that shows against its slot (3.05 : 1), so a customer who edits the key color has very little room. The print is a warm white, not pure white, at 10.6 to 12.1 : 1, because pure white on charcoal glares in a dark room. This is the one of the pair for a dark stage.

The same seven asks as Soft Sector, screen 25. None is new; this is what each one has to cover in the dark.

AskWhat changes in the dark
1 A legend at the top left. Only the ink: white on the gray key, 4.96 : 1 at the dark far edge of the dish, which is where the legend sits.
2 An LED in the corner. LampPosition Nothing. An LED would read bare on this case, but on a key it still needs the corner to stay clear of the legend.
3 A ridged knob. KnobKnurlCount The ridges alternate #7A7A7D and #3A3A3D, twice the spread of the flat top's own shading, because on a dark knob the ridges are most of what says there is an edge to grip.
4 A molded tray. The shadow inside a tray has little left to darken on charcoal, so the lit lower lip, 8 percent white, is what shows the tray. The ask has to name both, not only the shade.
5 Key travel. PressTravelPixels A glow reads a little better than on the putty, 1.69 : 1 against 1.13, and is still not a signal on its own. Travel keeps "on" from depending on color here too.
6 Smoked windows. Black acrylic is 1.21 : 1 from the case, so without the gloss a display is only a darker patch. The gloss is part of the ask, not decoration. Values in a window keep their slot's color, as on Soft Sector.
7 The keycap. SwitchShape The dark key is the neutral slot again, now black. The skirt is lit along its back edge, lighter than the top, because on charcoal a key is told from the case by light rather than by shadow.

What I left out on purpose. The tower in your photographs has a perforated mesh of round holes rather than slots; that is a pattern Soft Sector does not have, and you asked for its geometry, so the vents stay slots. The one nod to the photographs is the red wordmark: the model number beside the name is printed red, as a logo rather than a label, at 3.73 : 1. Blue LEDs were on the front of machines like these by the time they were black; the six slots stay Soft Sector's so a layout lights the same way in both. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the vents and the drive's door are line controls in a literal color, so they are the one thing on the page a theme change does not carry. This page sets them black, and a layout moved from Soft Sector keeps its putty-gray vents until somebody edits them.
