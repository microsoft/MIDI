Screen 25 — Soft Sector, the beige computer of the late eighties and early nineties: the 486 tower, the terminal and the keyboard in front of them, named for the floppy disks they read. Cathode, Terminal Amber and Terminal Green are the screen; this is the case. Putty plastic with a molded texture, charcoal print, and every button is a key with its legend at the top left and an LED in the corner. The only color on the page is those LEDs, red, amber and green, and anything that lights sits behind smoked plastic. A light theme, and the first where "on" is said by where a control is as well as by a light.

The stipplefine noise with no direction, light and dark, under the controls
Why a key says "on" with where it is. A glow has nowhere to go on a light case. The green halo that is the whole of "on" on a dark theme measures 1.13 : 1 against the key it sits on here, which is nothing. So this theme says it twice: the LED lights, in a black holder that keeps it at 4.36 to 10.89 : 1 whatever it is set into, and the key stays latched partway down, the way a turbo button did. The second needs no color vision at all, and that matters more on this palette than on any other: red, amber and green are the three colors a red-green color-blind eye confuses. Simulated, deuteranopia puts green and amber 9.1 apart where ordinary vision puts them 71. A light key's top is only 1.31 : 1 from the case, so as on Bone the separation is a warm shadow and the shading of the skirt, never value.

Built — what the engine already does for this case

The stipple is the deck grain drawn as specks (GrainPercent, GrainStreak 1) and the shadow is warm (ShadowColor). Every name, value, scale and pointer is the charcoal (InkColor, ValueColor, PointerColor, KnobTickCount 11, PointerOnCap). A slider is a slot straight in the case with no fill and a printed scale (FaderPlate none, FaderFillPercent 0, FaderScalePercent), under a light cap with a charcoal line (ThumbColor, CapLineColor). A switch that is on lights a round LED and keeps its color (FillWhenOnPercent 0, LampShape dot). The neutral slot is the dark key (NeutralCaps), and a neutral cap already lights the first slot's lamp, so its LED is green. The drive's faceplate is an inset panel (InsetPanelColor), labels sit above, and the meter runs green, amber, red (MeterSlots).

Needs — the key, and the window

The keycap is the theme. Buttons drawn as the keys of a keyboard of the period are what makes this read as a computer and not as a gray page, and today every switch is a flat plate. Key travel is what lets "on" be a position as well as a light. And anything that lights needs a window: today a meter or a readout stands on a plate like a key, where a green bar measures 1.1 : 1, and a value in the charcoal inside an XY field or an LFO's window is black on black. Four of the asks are small; those three are the theme.

What it costs

Color is an LED. Knobs and sliders are putty, gray and charcoal, so this theme cannot group knobs or sliders by color; a slot shows only in a lit LED, a window or the meter. Red and green. The palette is the worst case for red-green color vision: simulated, green and amber are 9.1 apart for deuteranopia and green and yellow 4.2 for protanopia. On is also the key's position, so nothing is carried by color alone, but the picker should say it. And it is a light theme: on a dark stage the putty is the brightest thing in the room. Every slot clears 5.30 : 1 in its window, and the closest two, red and orange, are delta E 27.9 apart.

Seven asks, smallest first. Each is independent, and the page above uses all seven.

AskWhy Soft Sector needs itWithout it
1 A legend at the top left. An inside top left label placement, and a theme default that puts every switch's name there. A key prints its legend in the top left corner, and a keypad key prints its other job under it. A name inside a switch is centered today, which is what a button does. Centered names: buttons, not keys.
2 An LED in the corner. LampPosition: top center, or top right. The LED on a lock key sits in its corner, clear of the legend. At the top center it runs into the legend on any key narrower than about 50 px. Lamp and legend fighting for the top edge.
3 A ridged knob. KnobKnurlCount: ridges round the side of a knob; zero is smooth. A monitor's brightness knob is a short ridged cylinder with a flat top. The ridges are what make a small knob read as something to grip rather than as a disc. A smooth disc.
4 A molded tray. The recess shade on a panel: shadow along its top inside edge and a lit lip along its bottom. A section is a shallow tray pressed into the case rather than paint on it. A translucent panel color already lets the stipple through; the recess is the part that is missing. Sections that look printed on a molded case.
5 Key travel. PressTravelPixels: how far a switch goes down under a finger. A latched switch sits halfway, and its shadow shortens with it. Zero is today. On is said by the LED and by the key staying down. The second needs no color vision and no glow, and a glow is 1.13 : 1 on this case. On is a small colored dot, and red or green is all it has to say.
6 Smoked windows. The well for everything that lights, meters, readouts, clocks and time displays as well as the fields that have one today, with a gloss on it. And a value inside a well keeps its slot's color where the theme prints values in ink. What lights sits behind dark plastic. ValueColor paints every value one color: charcoal on the case is right, and charcoal in a dark window cannot be seen. Either the windows go dark and empty, or every value is an LED color and the knobs vanish on the putty at 1.1 : 1.
7 The keycap. SwitchShape: plate or keycap. A skirt, and a top face set into it, inset more at the front than the back and dished, both worked out from the cap color, so the neutral slot's dark key comes free. It is the theme. The neutral cap today is a gradient lifted 45 percent toward white, which suits cream and would turn a dark modifier key nearly into the light one. Light gray buttons.

What I left out on purpose. The key lock, the floppy drive's latch lever and a maker's metal badge are decoration with no control behind them. A seven-segment face for the readouts is the most recognizable thing on the front of a 486, and it is a typeface, which belongs to the layout rather than the theme, so the windows use the readout's own font. The case is new rather than yellowed; a yellowed Soft Sector is one color change away if anybody asks. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the meter is drawn as LED segments, which the app has not drawn for any theme yet; the keypad keys carry two legends, and the app has one name per control, so the second is either a two-line name or left off. The reference photographs from the first session did not carry over to this one, so the colors are from memory of the hardware rather than sampled, and every one was then measured.
