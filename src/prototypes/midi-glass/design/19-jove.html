Screen 19 — Jove, after the Roland Jupiter-8. Matte black steel, one orange, and a row of colored tabs. This is the first theme where a control is not on a plate at all: a knob is a black cap sitting straight on the panel and a fader is a slot milled into it. Only the tab switches are objects, and a tab does not carry a color — it is one. That inverts how every other theme uses the six slots, and it is the one real strain this puts on the model.

The strain. Every theme before this one spends a slot on a hairline. Jove spends each of its six on a whole button face, at full saturation, at rest — which is the exact thing the “nothing is saturated at rest” rule exists to prevent. It gets away with it because the panel around them is #262626 and there are only ever a few dozen tabs, but it is the first theme that would genuinely be worse at a hundred and twenty controls, and the picker should say so.

Your seven colors fit — because one of them is not a color

Red, orange, yellow, green, aqua and blue are exactly six, which is exactly what a theme has. The cream tabs are the seventh only if you count them as a color; they are better read as the absence of one — a tab with no assignment. That is worth making real: a theme names one neutral beside its six hues, and a control can pick it. Studio Dark's neutral would be a gray, Bone's a warm white. One property, and it stops a customer reaching for a literal color and losing it at the next theme swap.

Built — what the engine now does for this panel

Fill at rest by control kind, so the tabs are solid and the knobs are black on the same panel. A pointer color and a cap line color. Then, from the engine round after this screen: every value — arc, fill, puck, LFO wave — in the section orange (ValueColor). A knob is a turned black cap lit above the middle, with its arc outside it (KnobFaceColor). A fader has no plate, a faint fill and a cap that stands off the slot with a wide white line (FaderPlate, FaderFillPercent, ThumbShadowPercent, CapLineWide). A tab is glossed and carries its name; lit, it goes paler and glows (PlateShadePercent, PlateHighlightPercent, NamesInsideSwitches, OnLiftPercent). An LFO sits in a well instead of being filled like a tab.

Built too — and what is still different

Labels sit above their control, and the section header is a filled bar over a section drawn as a faint outline (PanelFill none, PanelOutlineColor). Still different: the panel is one flat color rather than this screen's soft gradient; the bold, letter-spaced silkscreen is whatever font each label is set in; the banner has no bracket line under it; and the pointer is 2 px wide rather than 3.

On the name. Jove is the planet, not the instrument — same idea, nothing borrowed. Matte Steel and Tab Row both describe it if you would rather the name said what it looks like. Colors are eyeballed from your photograph rather than sampled from anything of Roland's, and every one of the six measures over 3:1 against the panel, which a saturated color on near-black does easily. What I left out on purpose: the instrument's silkscreen is dense enough to need a magnifier, and the arrows and brackets that tie a knob to its label are documentation rather than controls — a customer who wants that can draw it with text and rule objects, which are already surface objects. I also kept the fader slot faintly lit behind the cap. The real panel has nothing there at all, and the cap position is the whole value; a completely unlit slot reads correctly but it is the one place where being right about the instrument costs the language a rule, so it is yours to call.
