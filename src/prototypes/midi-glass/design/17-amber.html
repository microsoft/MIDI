Screen 17 — Amber Console, the P3 terminal. This is the swatch that has been sitting in the theme picker since screen 8, finally built. Three things make it what it is: the glass is a warm maroon rather than green, the glow is redder than the thing casting it because P3 decays through red, and brightness moves hue — the ramp runs like heat, from deep ember through orange and amber and yellow to white. That last one means this theme has something Cathode did not have at all: a second channel.

Free — what Amber reuses

Everything Cathode needed, already built for Cathode: the tonal machinery with the wash off, the six slots, the scan line overlay, the deck falloff, inverse video for on, and snow for a device that has gone. The second theme to want scan lines is what turns it from a one-off into a property — pitch and strength, one overlay for the whole deck. That argument was worth waiting for.

One addition — what it needs

The halo color has to stop being derived from the slot. Bone's white light and Cathode's blue-white were both the same color as the thing emitting, so one property covered it. Here they are different on purpose: the slot is amber and the halo is ember, because P3 decays through red. So bloom color becomes its own value rather than a shade of the hue. That is the third theme to ask for the same property, which is usually the point at which it stops being a special case.

Three things the measurement decided

The ramp works. Mean adjacent delta E 21.9 against Cathode's 6.5, smallest adjacent 13.6 against 5.3, widest pair 4.18:1 against 2.32. Amber can color code and Cathode could not. The bottom rung cannot. Ember is 2.65:1 as type and 4.10:1 under a dark label, so it carries light and never letters. The box still does not separate itself at 1.11:1 against the glass, exactly as on Cathode, so the glow is structure here too.

Nothing is pure here either. The palette runs #140B07 to #FFF3D2, and swept over the whole deck the darkest pixel is #120804 and the lightest is exactly #FFF3D2. That took one rule: every recess is the palette floor at an alpha rather than a dark picked by eye. Hand-picked darks bottomed the blue channel out at zero as soon as a scan line and the corner falloff were both laid over them, which is a pure black by another name. The one thing everybody remembers about P3 is the smear. It had a long red decay, which is why it was sold as the kind screen and why scrolling text left a trail. The trail is in here, but as drawing rather than animation: a fader's fill is the slot color at the leading edge and ember by the far end, and the XY pad's trail is the longest of any theme in the set. The feedback hold runs around 400 ms against Studio Dark's 220. Under the Windows reduced motion setting the decay becomes an instant change, the same as everywhere else. The theme does not pick a typeface. A monospace face is what everybody pictures here and the starter layout uses Cascadia Mono, but a font belongs to the layout — changing somebody's type because they chose a color scheme is overreach, and it would wreck a layout that was laid out around its labels. Where it belongs. A dark room, and the one retro theme that can still carry grouping by color, which makes it the better choice of the two for a big page. Next to Cathode it is warmer, louder and easier to read; Cathode is the quieter instrument.
