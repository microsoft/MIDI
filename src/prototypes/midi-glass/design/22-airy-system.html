Screen 22 — Airy System, after Roland's AIRA family: the synthesizers, the drum machine and the mixer. Black brushed metal and green light, and it is meant to be loud. The first theme that lights three kinds of control three different ways at rest: a knob sits in a ring of its own light and a fader in a lit frame, all the time; a button is black until it is on and then its edge glows; and a drum pad is colored plastic, dim until it plays. The mixer spends the same palette on its buttons, one job to a color.

Why three rest states. Every theme so far says what "at rest" looks like once, for the whole page. This panel says it three ways, and each is the instrument's own. Draw a button like a fader and every button glows all the time, so turning one on is a smaller change than it should be. Draw a pad like a button and the drum machine's colors only show on the steps that are playing. Both are one more number on something the engine already sets by kind — SwitchFillAtRest is the precedent, from Jove.

Built — what the engine already does for this panel

A knob is a black cap (KnobFaceColor) with a white pointer (PointerColor) inside its arc. A fader is a lit frame: the rim in the control's color near full strength (RimStrengthPercent), its light spilling at rest (RestingGlowPercent), a gray cap and a white line (ThumbColor, CapLineColor). A lit button keeps its black and its edge comes up in its own color with a glow (FillWhenOnPercent about 12). Names sit inside switches (NamesInsideSwitches). The scatter box and the page frame are sections drawn as a line of green (PanelFill none, PanelOutlineColor), named in green (SectionNameInHue). The meter is green, yellow and red (MeterSlots).

Needs — three rest states, and a brushed panel

A switch rests dark while a knob and a fader stay lit. Today the resting rim and the resting glow are one number each for the whole theme, so either every button glows at rest or no fader frame does. A pad is its own family: dim colored plastic at rest and its color outright when lit, on the same page as buttons that are black with a lit edge. And the panel is brushed: long streaks along one direction, where the grain today can only draw specks.

What it costs

This is a light show, on purpose. Everything that turns or slides glows all the time, so on a dense page most of the surface is light, and a touch has less to stand out against than it does on Studio Dark. It is for a dark room, and the picker should say so. A glow never carries a state on its own: a lit button's edge goes from nothing to its full color, 5.6 : 1 or better against the deck on every slot, so somebody who cannot see a glow still sees the edge arrive. The closest two slots, red and orange, are delta E 41.7 apart.

Six asks, smallest first. Each is independent, and the page above uses all six.

AskWhy Airy System needs itWithout it
1 A section name centered. A fourth header style beside caption, filled bar and notched. The scatter box names itself centered across its top, and so does every section on the synthesizers. Five-iSH, screen 21, asks for the same thing. The name at the top left.
2 A brushed grain. One number on the grain that is there today: how long a speck is, along the page. One is a speck; forty or more is a brushed streak. Brushed metal is fine streaks running one way. Same tile, same one brush for the whole deck, nothing per control. Sandpaper, like Supersaw, or a flat black.
3 The arc glows. A knob's arc gets the halo a fader's fill already has, on a theme that asks for it, so nothing shipped moves. Here the ring is the light source. Screen 9 has drawn a glowing arc since the first comp; the app draws a flat one on every theme. A ring that is painted, not lit.
4 A ring in the control's own color. The unlit part of the arc can be the control's own hue at a strength, instead of one track color for every knob. A knob sits in a ring of its own light. With one track color, a blue knob sits in a green ring. Every ring one color, so a dim gray.
5 A switch rests dark. A resting rim and a resting glow for switches, apart from knobs and faders. Below zero means follow the theme, which is what every shipped theme does. Knobs and faders are lit at rest; a button is black until it is on. It is fill at rest by kind again, for the rim and the glow. Every button glows at rest and on is a smaller change, or no fader frame is lit.
6 A pad is its own family. Fill at rest and fill when on for pads, apart from buttons and toggles. The steps are colored plastic, dim at rest and full when lit, beside black buttons with lit edges. One fill for every switch cannot draw both. The solid style can do it one pad at a time today, but then it follows the pad into every other theme. Pads drawn like buttons: the drum machine's colors only show on the steps that are playing.

What I left out on purpose. The drum machine lights one numbered lamp of ten round its scatter knob to show one position; the app's lamp ring lights everything up to the value, so this screen prints the ten positions as marks instead. The instrument names under every step, the bass synthesizer's touch pad and the family's logo are left out as well. The white name tags are labels with a solid style and a black name, which the app can already draw one label at a time. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the green and the four step colors are the family's own; blue and violet are mine, picked to glow the same way, so the mixer has six jobs to give colors to. Colors are eyeballed from your photographs rather than sampled from anything of Roland's, and every one was then measured.
