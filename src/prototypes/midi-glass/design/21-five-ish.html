Screen 21 — Five-iSH, after the Roland SH-7, and the look it shares with the SH-5 and the modular systems of the same years. Black metal, and a panel printed in two layers: each section is a tan block with its name in black across the top, and the controls sit on a green block inside it, with their names and scales in white. Knobs are silver with a black cap; sliders are black, with one row in cream. This is the first theme with two surfaces and two inks on one page, and nothing on it is colored except the lamps.

Inkneither one can do both surfaces
Why the second ink is not a detail. Every theme so far prints every name in one ink, picked against the plate. This panel prints on two surfaces a long way apart in value — the tan measures 9.8 : 1 against the metal — so the print has to change with the surface under it. White on the tan is 1.5 : 1 and black on the green is 3.0 : 1. The renderer already knows where every control sits to lay it out. Knowing what a label sits on as well is the same question, and so is drawing a green panel inside a tan one.

Built — what the engine already does for this panel

A knob is a silver skirt with a black cap (KnobFaceColor, KnobCapColor, KnobCapSizePercent) and a printed scale of eleven marks (KnobTickCount). Every value — arc, fill, puck, LFO wave — is the print white (ValueColor), and so is every pointer and cap line. A fader is a slot cut straight into the panel with a black cap standing off it (FaderPlate none, ThumbShadowPercent, RecessShadePercent). A switch lights a lamp in its slot's color and leaves the cap black (FillWhenOnPercent 0). A section is tan (PanelFill color), labels sit above, and the meter is green, yellow and red (MeterSlots).

Needs — the second layer and the second ink

A panel inside a panel. Tan and green are two colors and a theme has one panel color today, so every section comes out the same and there is nowhere for the controls to sit. Ink by surface. One ink cannot do both: the print white measures 1.5 : 1 on the tan and black 3.0 : 1 on the green. Both ask one question — what is under this? — and between them they are most of what makes this look like the instrument rather than like a dark theme with a beige box on it.

What it costs

Color is a lamp. Knobs and faders are all silver, black and white, so this theme cannot group knobs by color; a slot shows only when a switch lights or a meter moves. Controls belong on the green. A white value on the tan is 1.5 : 1 and the lamp red on the green is 1.7 : 1, so values stay on the green or the metal and lamps stay on their caps. The picker should say both. Every lamp clears 4.9 : 1 against its cap, and the closest two, amber and yellow, are delta E 33.5 apart.

Seven asks, smallest first. Each is independent, and the page above uses all seven.

AskWhy Five-iSH needs itWithout it
1 Printed fader scales. No new property: a theme that prints a scale round every knob prints a fader's marks the same way. The instrument prints a scale beside every slider as boldly as round every knob. Today a fader's marks are the faint ones meant for inside a plate. Barely visible marks beside the sliders.
2 A round lamp. LampShape: bar or dot. A panel lamp of this era is a round lens. The bar is right for the JP-8000 and wrong here. A short bar across the top of the cap.
3 The pointer on the cap. The line is drawn over the cap rather than under it, from the cap's edge to near its middle. The white line is printed on the black cap, the part that turns. Today the cap covers the pointer's inner end, so here the line would be a short stub on the silver, white on silver. A knob that is hard to read at a glance.
4 A section name centered. A fourth header style beside caption, filled bar and notched. Each section's name is centered across the top of its tan block. Airy System, screen 22, asks for the same thing. The name at the top left.
5 Cream caps on the neutral slot. A fader or a switch on the neutral slot wears the theme's neutral as its cap. Black and cream are both the instrument's colors, and the one cream row is how it says "these five belong together". It is the neutral slot's job: the absence of a color, not a seventh one. Every cap black.
6 A panel inside a panel. A second panel color and end color, for a panel that sits inside another one. Printed flat: no shadow. The two layers are the theme. All tan or all green.
7 Ink by surface. A section ink, for anything printed on a section rather than on an inset or the deck. Neither ink can do both surfaces: 1.5 : 1 or 3.0 : 1. Every name on the tan set by hand in a literal color, which the next theme swap leaves behind.

What I left out on purpose. The printed numbers round every knob and beside every scale are on the big specimens only; on a real page they are text a customer can add, and at deck size they are noise. The arrows and waveform symbols that tie a switch to what it selects are documentation rather than controls. The instrument's lever switches have no equivalent in the app, so a two or three way switch is drawn as buttons with lamps, one lit. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the instrument has one lamp color, red, and a lamp or a meter is the only place a slot can show here, so slots two to six are the other colors a panel lamp came in — amber, yellow, green, blue and a warm clear. Colors are eyeballed from your photographs rather than sampled from anything of Roland's, and every one was then measured.
