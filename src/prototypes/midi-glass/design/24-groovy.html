Why goldenrodthe same cream plate on the two backgrounds you offered
Why the lines are heavy. A flat theme has nothing else to hold a control apart from the page: no shadow under it, no glow around it, and the cream plate measures only 1.41 : 1 against the gold. At the one pixel rim every theme draws today, a button is a pale patch with a thread around it. At three pixels the line becomes the edge, the way the shadow is the edge on Bone. Everything else is drawn at the same weight — the knob's band, the pointer, the lane and the stripes — so the page reads as one piece of print.

Built — what the engine already does for this panel

A flat page and flat plates: no sheen, no shadow, no glow (PlateSheenPercent, PlateElevation, GlowStrength all 0). A cream plate (PlateColor) with its rim in the control's color at full strength (RimStrengthPercent 100). On is the color outright (FillWhenOnPercent 100), and a pad carries a third of it at rest (PadFillAtRest). Under a finger a knob's face takes a fifth of its color (TouchFillPercent); a switch says it is held by turning on. A cream knob face (KnobFaceColor), a brown pointer (PointerColor), and cream wherever a lane, a band or a field is empty (TrackColor, WellColor). A fader with no plate, a brown cap with a wide cream line, and a scale printed beside the lane (FaderPlate none, ThumbColor, CapLineColor, CapLineWide, FaderScalePercent). Brown as the neutral (NeutralColor). Sections as brown outlines named in a notch (PanelFill none, PanelOutlineColor, SectionHeader notched), and avocado, ochre and red on the meter (MeterSlots).

Needs — heavier lines, stripes and two colors

Two colors the engine works out for itself today: the name on a lit switch, and what a pad's resting color is mixed into. Every rim is one pixel and every knob arc four; here a rim is three and a knob's band six, with a four pixel pointer and a ten pixel lane, and a knob's band has round ends, the way a stripe folds back on itself. And a section's outline and a rule are a band of stripes: three flat colors side by side, each corner concentric with the one outside it. Without the stripes the theme still works; it just stops looking like the seventies.

What it costs

This is a light theme, and a bright one. A gold page is for a lit room; in a dark one it is the brightest thing there, and the picker should say so. A state never depends on color: on is a cream plate turning solid, which anybody can see. But color is the only thing that tells two slots apart. All six sit within 1.43 : 1 of each other in brightness, so without color vision the page is one brown; name the controls, and group them by place rather than by color. Every slot clears 3.0 on the page and 4.5 as type on the cream. The closest two, burnt orange and red, are delta E 23.3 apart.

Five asks, smallest first. Each is independent, and the page above uses all five.

AskWhy Groovy needs itWithout it
1 A light ink. A second ink, for the name on a switch that is on. A theme names one ink today, and where that is too dark the engine picks its own near-white, #F2F3F5. The ink here is dark brown, only 2.60 : 1 on a lit burnt orange switch, so every lit name falls back to that cool gray-white. The cream #FFF7E6 is delta E 10.3 warmer, and on a page of earth tones the cool one is what looks wrong. Lit names in a cool gray-white.
2 A pad's resting color on the plate. Mix a pad's color at rest into the plate instead of the page, for a theme that asks, so no other theme changes. Mixed into the gold, every resting pad turns a shade of mustard: teal comes out olive #AEAD68 and denim khaki #B5A66B. Mixed into the cream they are the pale peach, pink and sage of the period, and they match every other control. Pads rest as darker patches of the gold.
3 Round ends. A knob's band, the value and the empty part both, ends in a round cap instead of a square cut. The pointer already has one. The shapes of the period are soft: a pill, a disc, a stripe that folds back on itself. A square end on a thick band reads as a gauge. Square ends, so a knob looks like an instrument rather than a poster.
4 Heavier lines. Two numbers: how thick a rim is (one pixel today) and how thick a knob's band is (four). The pointer and a fader's lane follow the band, and a section's outline follows the rim. You asked for thicker lines, and on a flat page they are the structure. The cream plate is 1.41 : 1 against the gold, so the outline is the only edge a control has. Hairlines. A button is a pale patch with a thread around it.
5 Stripes. A section's outline and a rule can be a band of two to four flat stripes in colors the theme names. Each corner stays concentric with the one outside it, the innermost turns the same curve as a control, and a section's name is cut through all of them. The bent stripe is the thing everybody recognizes from the period, and a section frame is where it goes: every section turns its corners like the posters. It is the outline the engine already draws, drawn three times, each one inside the last. One brown line, as heavy as a rim.

What I left out on purpose. The big shapes that fill a poster from the period — the sunburst, the flowers, the wavy bands — belong to a layout rather than a theme, and a layout can already put a picture behind a page. The paper grain of an old print is left off because you asked for no shading. The title is set in Cooper Black, which comes with Microsoft Office rather than with Windows; that is the layout's choice, and the theme sets no typeface. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the palette you sent leads with orange #E5771E, and on the gold it measures 1.86 : 1, so it lives in the stripes and the default slot is burnt orange #B34A1A, which clears 3.0. The page is #ECC864, a step lighter than your #E6C15C (delta E 2.6), for the same reason. Teal, denim and red are deepened the same way, and every color was measured.
