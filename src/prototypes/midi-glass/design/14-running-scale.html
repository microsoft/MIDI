A layout opens at the size it was designed at and scrolls if the window is smaller, because that is the only mode where a control is exactly the size the person who built it intended. Fit to screen is one press away and scales 1:1 on both axes, letterboxed. Whichever of the three a layout was last left in is what it opens in next time. Full screen carries no bar at all — just one small button, in the corner the customer picked.

One button, and it can move. Full screen has no bar, no strip and no reserved edge — the deck runs to all four sides. The only chrome is a 34 px button that sits in whichever corner the layout says, so it can be put where that particular rig has nothing important. It fades to about a quarter opacity a few seconds after the last touch and comes back on any pointer movement or a tap in that corner. The three dotted squares here are the other corners, shown only to make the setting obvious in this mockup. Panic is also on a hardware key and is the one item in the flyout that is always at the bottom, always the same color, and never moves — muscle memory matters more than tidiness for that one.
