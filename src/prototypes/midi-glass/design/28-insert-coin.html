Why the buttons carry their color at rest. An arcade button is colored plastic, lit or not, and a player finds the snare by its color long before reading a name. So at rest each button shows its color with no light behind it: 1.86 to 4.83 : 1 against the panel, enough to find, too dark to mistake for lit. Lit, it measures 1.93 to 2.56 : 1 brighter than the same button unlit, and it throws a halo. Held, it is also two pixels down. A player with no color vision still sees which buttons are on, because on is brighter and deeper, not only redder.

Built — what the engine already does for this panel

A black panel with a reflection across it, the sheet of clear plastic (faceplateSheenPercent). Colored plastic at rest: a switch's color mixed into a near-black plate (switchFillAtRest 0.62), and the color outright when on (fillWhenOnPercent 100) with a glow (glowStrength). Held means down: two pixels of travel (pressTravelPixels 2). A ring of eleven lights round every knob (valueIndicator segmentedLamps, lampCount 11). Fader caps in their color (thumb hue). Names printed below (labels below). Sections named on a bar of their color in whichever ink reads (sectionHeader filledBar). Meters, readouts and the XY field as dark windows (wellFillsControl), LEDs in black holders (lampShape dot, lampHolderColor), and green, yellow and red on the meter (meterSlots).

Needs — round buttons, a cap and a ball

Every switch is a rounded rectangle today. Here a button is a circle in a ring, the largest that fits, domed, with a hard highlight, and only the button moves when it is pressed. A knob's cap is one color for the whole theme; here it is the control's own color, with a pointer that stays readable on a yellow cap. The joystick's puck is flat; here it is a ball on a shaft. Good Form, screen 32, asks for the same round button and the same colored cap, so building them once pays twice.

What it costs

This page is colorful when nothing is happening. Every button carries its color all the time. That is right for a page of pads and a lot for a page of two hundred switches. Round buttons want square controls: a wide button is a circle in the middle of its box, with the sides left empty. Blue and purple are the closest pair: delta E 38 for most people, 11.5 for a green-blind (deutan) player and 5.4 for a red-blind (protan) one, so do not use those two for opposite jobs. A state never depends on color: lit is brighter and held is also down. Every lit color clears 3.58 : 1 on the panel, and the print 15.28.

Three asks, smallest first. Each is independent, and the page above uses all three.

AskWhy Insert Coin needs itWithout it
1 Knob caps in the control's color. A theme can ask for each knob's cap to take the control's own color, instead of one cap color for every knob. The pointer printed on the cap picks white or near-black, whichever reads on that cap, the way a switch's name does. On the controllers this is based on, and on the colored caps players fit to them, a knob's color is its cap. Here the cap says which knob it is from across the room, and the ring of lights says where it is set. Every cap one color. The knob's color shows only in its ring of lights.
2 A ball on the joystick. The joystick's puck drawn as a glossy ball in the control's color on a short shaft, over a dark washer, with its shadow falling down and to the right. A stick you push should look like one you can grab. The ball is the part of an arcade cabinet everybody recognizes, and it shows where the stick is pushed by where it sits over the washer. The usual flat puck, a small cap with a dot of color.
3 Round buttons. A button, toggle, pad or lamp drawn as the largest circle that fits its control, centered, in a ring of a color the theme names, and domed, with a hard highlight at the upper left. At rest it shows the switch fill at rest; on, the color outright with a lighter center; held, it also moves down by the press travel the engine already has, and the ring stays put. The round button is the whole idea. A rounded rectangle in these colors reads as a toy keyboard; a circle in a ring reads as a cabinet, and the ring is what holds a dark button apart from a black panel. Colored rounded rectangles. It still works; it just stops looking like an arcade.

What I left out on purpose. The artwork printed on a cabinet's panel belongs to a layout, and a layout can already put a picture behind a page. The rings of light on these controllers often pulse and chase in patterns; a pulsing light carries no information here and is a hazard for some players, so a light is on or off. The type is Bahnschrift, which ships with Windows and has the condensed capitals a cabinet prints in; that is the layout's choice, and the theme sets no typeface. Where I was honest rather than faithful: real arcade buttons have no names, and here every button's name is printed under it, because a layout is often played by somebody who did not build it. The blue is a step lighter than a stock arcade blue so it clears 3.58 : 1 on the black, and white print on it 5.00. Every color was measured.
