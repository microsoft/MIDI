Built — what the engine already does for this panel

Everything Groovy's card lists, with the colors turned over: an espresso plate and knob face (PlateColor, KnobFaceColor), espresso lanes, bands and fields (TrackColor, WellColor), a cream pointer and a cream fader cap with an espresso line (PointerColor, ThumbColor, CapLineColor), cream as the neutral (NeutralColor), and cream print (InkColor). Plus the paper grain: fine specks on the page, under the controls (GrainPercent, GrainStreak 1, GrainColor unset, so the specks come from the page itself).

Needs — Groovy's five asks, and nothing else

The ink for a lit switch is a dark one here: the name on a lit switch is espresso, #2A1911, where today the engine would pick its own near-black, #101114. Pads tint the espresso plate, the knob's band has round ends, the lines are three and six pixels, and the band is four stripes of three pixels instead of three of four. The same code draws both themes; only the values differ.

What it costs

A dark theme, for a dark room. At rest the page is quiet, but a switch that is on is a solid block of color, so a page with many switches on gets loud: keep the colored switches to the ones that matter. Color is what tells the slots apart: all six sit within 1.79 : 1 of each other in brightness. With red-green color blindness, red and avocado are only delta E 8.1 apart and orange and gold 12, so don't use either pair for two switches that mean opposite things. Every slot clears 4.0 on the page and 4.66 as type on the plate.

The same five asks as Groovy, with this theme's values. Nothing is new, and the grain is built.

Grain, already built. Fine specks from the page's own color. Measured on this render: the page varies by about 5 levels (one standard deviation) and its average does not move. None: flat, as you asked for Groovy.

What I left out on purpose. The grunge and halftone dots of your second picture are heavier than paper, and a fine speck is what the engine's grain draws. The grain sits on the page and under the controls, the way the engine lays it; the poster's grain covers the stripes as well, which would need a new feature. The big shapes — the S bend, the sunburst, the diagonal — belong to a layout, which can already put a picture behind a page. Where I was honest rather than faithful: mint and sage from your palettes aren't among the six, so each slot stays in the same family as Groovy's — orange, avocado, teal, gold, denim and red — and a layout switched between the two themes keeps its meaning.
