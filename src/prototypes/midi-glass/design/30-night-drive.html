Built — what the engine already does for this road

A picture for the deck, fitted to the page without stretching, so the sun stays round (deck image). Dark glass plates and sections (glassColor, glassTintPercent 90, panelFill color). Neon: glow, arcs that glow (arcGlow), a white-hot core down every value (valueCorePercent) and words that glow in their own color (neonLetters). Pads that light up in their color with dark violet ink (padFillWhenOnPercent 100, onInkColor), and switches that light only their edge (fillWhenOnPercent 0). Section names cut into the top line (sectionHeader notched). Readouts and the XY field as dark windows (wellFillsControl). Meter zones in mint, sun and pink (meterSlots). A warning in the picker (cautionResourceKey).

Needs — a halo, chrome, and a road that moves

Words on the deck have nothing behind them, so on a bright picture they can vanish. Here they get a soft halo. A cap has two colors and a smooth blend between them; chrome needs a hard line through the middle, which two colors cannot make. Names are ink or neon; here the section names are chrome too. The grid is part of the picture, so it stands still; the engine would have to draw it to make it move. That last one is optional.

What it costs

A picture is a lot to put behind a control surface. The sun pulls the eye to the middle, so a layout with its controls in the middle hides the best part. The horizon is bright: 2.43 : 1 for white print. Every word here sits on glass; until ask 1, the warning in the picker says to keep words off the horizon. It is a night theme, made for a dark room. The six neons are the easiest set in this round for color-blind players: the closest pair is sun and orange, delta E 20.7 for a green-blind (deutan) player, and orange and mint, 33.2 for a red-blind (protan) one. A lit switch takes dark violet ink on every color, 4.94 to 12.88 : 1.

Four asks, smallest first. Each is independent; the last is optional.

AskWhy Night Drive needs itWithout it
1 A halo behind words on the deck. Anything printed straight on the deck, rather than on a plate or a section, gets a soft halo in a color the theme names. Here it is the sky's darkest violet at 65 percent. A picture has bright places, and a customer can put a Text control anywhere. White on the bare horizon is 2.43 : 1; on the halo it is 9.08. Offworld and every customer's own picture get the same protection. The warning in the picker says to keep words off the horizon, and the layout's author has to.
2 Chrome caps. Knob caps and fader caps drawn in chrome: a gradient with a hard line through the middle, sky above it and dark ground with a warm reflection below, in stops the theme names. Chrome is half of this look, and the hard horizon line is what makes metal read as polished. Two colors blended smoothly read as plastic. A smooth light to dark cap, from the two cap colors the engine has.
3 Chrome letters. Section names and the words on a Text control filled with the same chrome, in italic, on a dark badge so the dark line through them never meets a dark sky. The chrome lettering is the other half of the look, and it is what a title in this style is. The names in neon, which the engine already does.
4 A moving floor, optional. The engine draws the grid itself, in the picture's perspective, and rolls it slowly toward the viewer. It stands still when Windows asks for fewer animations, the way the rain does. The grid sliding toward the sun is what makes it a drive. It would be the only thing on the page that moves on its own, which is why it is optional and last. The grid in the picture, standing still. Only the motion is lost.

What I left out on purpose. Palm trees, mountains and a car. They are pictures of things, and a deck picture should be a place, not a scene; a customer can still use their own. The name: this sunset belongs to nobody, and I kept away from the name of the arcade game that made it famous. The type in the title is the layout's choice: Segoe UI Variable in chrome, and Segoe Script in neon, both in Windows. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the real look puts chrome on everything. Here chrome is only on what you grab, so it always means "hold this", and every value is neon, so it always means "this is where it is". Every color was measured.
