Screen 20 — Supersaw, after the Roland JP-8000. A matte blue panel with the texture of very fine sandpaper, shiny black molding, and one small red lamp per switch. Two things here that no theme before it has asked for: the panel has a grain — every deck so far has been a color, a gradient or a photograph — and on is not a plate state. A switch that is on looks exactly like a switch that is off, except for three lit pixels above its label.

Why the lamp matters more than it looks. A rack of sixteen identical black switches with one lamp lit is far easier to read at arm's length than sixteen colored blocks, because the eye only has to find the bright thing. Every theme before this one fills the plate when a control is on, so there is currently no way to say “do not”. It is one value on a property that already exists rather than a new one — and the same switch is the answer to the other question this panel raises, which is what a toggle should look like when there are forty of them.

Built — the grain

A deck can be a color, a gradient or an image, and now a flat color with a fine grain over it (GrainPercent). The grain is most of why the blue reads as a manufactured object rather than a fill. It is a tile — one brush over the whole deck, drawn once, nothing per control — because an image is stretched to the page and a stretched noise tile is blur.

Built — on without a fill

At zero fill when on, a switch lights one red lamp and leaves the plate black (FillWhenOnPercent, LampColor); lit, a faint line of that red goes round it. The value strip that used to run along the top as well is gone, because a strip and a lamp was two lights saying one thing.

Built — knobs, faders and sections

A knob is a black turned body with a small light cap, a white pointer and a ring of printed marks outside its arc (KnobFaceColor, KnobCapColor, KnobTickCount). A fader is a slot in a strip of molding, sunk, with a cap wider than the strip that casts a shadow (FaderPlate strip, RecessShadePercent, ThumbShadowPercent). A section is a faint outline with its orange name in a gap cut into the top (SectionHeader notched, SectionNameInHue). Labels sit above their controls. Still different: the panel is one flat blue under the grain rather than this screen's soft gradient.

On the name. Supersaw is what the instrument is remembered for rather than what it is called, so it says the right thing without borrowing anything. Grit Blue is the alternative if you would rather the name described the surface. Where I was honest rather than faithful: the real panel has almost no color on it at all — orange section type, red switch lamps, a green display, and that is the lot. Three slots is not enough for somebody building a forty-control page, so slots four, five and six are mine: a steel blue, a pale amber and a violet, chosen to sit on that blue rather than taken off the instrument. Colors are eyeballed from your photographs, not sampled from anything of Roland's, and all six measure over 3:1 against the panel. What I left out: the dense list silkscreen beside the waveform selectors, which is documentation rather than controls, and the keyboard, which the app has as its own control already.
