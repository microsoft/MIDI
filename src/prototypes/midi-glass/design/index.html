MIDI Glass — design mockups

A customizable MIDI control surface for Windows, in the Windows MIDI Services tool family. Thirty-two screens, plus the design notes in MIDI-Glass-design.md and the sequenced build plan in MIDI-Glass-implementation-plan.md. Start with screen 9 if you want the visual language first, or screen 1 if you want the customer journey.

1Library
What opens when the app starts. Favorites, recents, thumbnails drawn from the layout, Run as the primary action.
2Running a layout, windowed
A six channel mixer, clip pads, XY pad, beat clock and FX, live. WinUI chrome above, the customer's deck below.
3Full screen
Borderless, the fading "press Esc" toast, and a DJ layout in a second theme. Its auto-hide bar is superseded by the corner button on screen 14.
4The editor
Palette, page, inspector, page rail and a live message monitor. Edit and Try are one keystroke apart.
5Arranging and Repeat
Magnetic guides, spacing pills, and the Repeat tool that builds a bank and steps the binding as it goes.
6What a control sends
Three messages, two devices, one control. Learn, feedback binding, and automatic MIDI 2.0 to 1.0 fold-down.
7Sequences
SysEx, notes with a length, waits and a repeat block. Why this is a list and not a script engine.
8Themes
Six hue slots, a deck, control defaults, measured contrast, and a live preview.
9The surface control language
Every control type at rest and in use. The plate, the rim, the light pipe and the bloom.
10Pages, layers and devices
Page order, a shared band, layer keys, and the device table that makes a layout portable.
11Two tonal themes
Pigment Light and Pigment Dark. Flat opaque plates, color carried by a tonal wash instead of a glow. No new rendering layers.
12Bigwig
Charcoal and gray, one lead orange and five section colors. Knobs are graded caps inside a thin arc, and a display is cut into the surface rather than raised off it.
13The canvas
Page size templates, the virtual canvas bigger than the page, off-page controls, and the two resize paths.
14Running: scale and chrome
Actual size, fit to screen and custom, saved per layout. Full screen with one corner button instead of a bar.
15Bone
A warm light theme on #E3DAC9 bone and #8A795D shadow. Depth comes from a soft warm shadow instead of value, and activity lights up white.
16Cathode
An old black and white television. Green glass, blue-white phosphor, soft edges, no pure black or white, and no color at all to tell one control from another.
17Amber Console
The P3 amber terminal. Warm glass, a halo redder than the thing casting it, and a ramp that runs like heat — so unlike Cathode it can still carry color.
18Terminal Green
The green screen, and the glass is not green — it is cool blue-slate. The third retro theme, and the first that needed no new theme property at all.
19Jove
Matte black steel, one orange, and a row of colored tabs. The first theme where a control sits on no plate at all, and where a tab does not carry a color — it is one.
20Supersaw
A grained blue panel and shiny black molding. The panel has a texture, and a switch that is on looks exactly like one that is off except for three lit pixels.
21Five-iSH
After the Roland SH-7. Black metal and a panel printed in two layers, tan sections with green insets, so it needs two inks on one page. Silver knobs with black caps, and color only in the lamps.
22Airy System
After Roland's AIRA family. Brushed black and green light: knobs and faders lit all the time, buttons dark until they are on, and drum pads that are colored plastic.
23Off-world Colonies
After the film Blade Runner. A wall of cast concrete blocks, stained and still wet, with rain over it, dark worn metal bolted to it and neon for light. The first theme whose wall is a repeated pattern, and the first whose lights flare.
24Groovy
After 1970s graphic design. A goldenrod page, cream controls and brown print in earth tones, and sections framed in bands of stripes. Nothing glows and nothing casts a shadow, so every control is held apart by a heavy line of its own color.
25Soft Sector
The beige computer of the late eighties: putty plastic, charcoal print, and every button a key with its legend at the top left and an LED in the corner. The only color on the page is those LEDs.
26Hard Sector
Soft Sector's machine in charcoal: gray keys with white legends and the same six LEDs. The same geometry and the same asks; only the colors change.
27Groovy Dark
Groovy on a brown page with a paper grain, for a dark room. Espresso controls ringed in their colors, cream print, and sections framed in four stripes of red, orange, gold and cream. The same engine features as Groovy, with different values.
28Insert Coin
Arcade panels and the finger drumming controllers built from their buttons. Round domed buttons in black rings, colored plastic at rest and lit when on, knob caps in their colors, and a joystick with a ball on top.
29Chicago
Windows 95. A teal desktop, gray windows with title bars in their color, and two pixel bevels: raised to press, pressed when down, sunken to hold a value. A latched switch is checkered.
30Night Drive
The synthwave sunset as a deck picture: a striped sun, a pink grid, dark glass, neon for values and chrome for what you hold. Words never sit on the bright part of the sky.
31Visor
A head-up display, first proposed as Telemetry. Thin light on a dark field: every control is four corners, closed only when it is on, with fine scales, chevrons and a reticle.
32Good Form
Rational industrial design and the small instruments that took it up again. A light gray panel, light keys that take their color when on, knobs whose caps are their color, and black windows.

Serve with pwsh -File serve.ps1 and open http://127.0.0.1:8742/. These files live in src/prototypes/midi-glass/design/ so the design record is versioned. Nothing in that folder ships.
