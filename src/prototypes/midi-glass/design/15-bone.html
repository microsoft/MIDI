Screen 15 — Bone. A warm light theme built out of two colors: #E3DAC9 for the space and #8A795D for everything that has to read as dark. Every other theme tells a control from its deck by value — a dark plate on a darker deck, or the reverse. Bone cannot: a warm white plate measures 1.23:1 against a bone deck, which is nothing. So the separation is carried by a soft warm shadow, and the thing that lights up is white instead of the control's color.

What the numbers said. A warm white plate measures 1.23:1 to 1.39:1 against a bone deck. There is no value difference to work with, so the soft warm shadow under every plate is not decoration here — it is the only thing telling a control from the space behind it. Drag elevation to zero and you do not get a flatter theme, you get a blank sheet.

And white light is a weak signal. White on bone is 1.39:1. A halo on its own cannot carry a state on a light deck, so the rim comes up to full color in the same moment. Anyone who cannot see the glow still sees the outline change.

Free — what Bone reuses

The same tonal machinery as Pigment and Bigwig with the hue wash turned off, the six color slots, the track color, corner rounding, and the value strip along the bottom edge where Studio Dark puts it. No new control types and no new shapes. Even the segmented LED ring comes along, because it is a mask over the arc that is already there.

Two additions — what it needs

A shadow color, because a black shadow on a bone deck goes a dirty gray and this theme lives or dies on that shadow being warm. And a light source for the bloom — the control's own color today, or white — which is the same shape of property as the rim source Bigwig asked for. The elevation shadow itself is no longer a cost: the renderer already draws a masked shadow under every plate (PlateElevation) and the dark themes simply keep it low. Bone turns it up and tints it.

Four things the measurement decided

Ochre at readout size is 3.82:1 on bone and a number needs 4.5, so readouts are ink, not color. Shadow cannot carry warm white at 4.5 either, so the filled state deepens 16 % and lands at 5.5:1. Plate against deck is 1.23:1, which is why the shadow is structural. White against bone is 1.39:1, which is why the rim always moves with the light. None of those four were taste.

Where this theme belongs, and where it does not. Bone is for a desk by a window, a classroom, a studio with the lights on — somewhere a near-black surface is the thing that looks wrong. In a dark room it is a lamp pointed at the performer, and Studio Dark still wins there; the theme picker should say so rather than letting somebody find out during a set. One rule it inherits and keeps. A control still never uses more than one color, and that color still only appears on the rim, the value and the fill. What changed is where the depth comes from: the other themes make a control by darkening it, Bone makes one by lifting it. Bone and Shadow are yours; the other four slots are mine. All six are measured against both the plate and the deck at 3:1 or better, and the deck is bone at the bottom and a lifted bone at the top, so the space is never anything else.
