Screen 12 — Bigwig, revisited. The first pass was one gray and one orange, and next to the product it came out flat. What the product does is a ladder of grays — the page, a section, a control, and a dark display cut into them — with knobs that are graded caps inside a thin arc, and color only on the value, the state and the name of a section. The knob cap and all four asks at the bottom are now built, and the app draws Bigwig from these values. The comparison panel still shows the app from before that round, because that is what it was drawn to compare against.

Free — theme data only

Six slots instead of one orange six times. The page and the plate named at both ends. A dark edge and a lit top instead of a light outline. The solid arc instead of the ring, and no value strip. A gray fader cap with a line of the control's color, and a fill that falls away below the value. Ink #C9C9C9, pointer #E6E6E6, and on at full strength.

Built — the knob face

A face color and an end color, lit above the middle (KnobFaceColor, KnobFaceEndColor). Every knob's arc now hangs outside its face, on every theme, with a 2 px gap. The pointer runs from just inside the face's edge toward the middle. Under a finger the face keeps its shading and takes the touch wash over it.

What it costs

Red is light, not letters. It measures 3.1 : 1 as a section name, so it carries a fill, an arc or the top of a meter, and never a name. The other five clear 4.5. The grays are close on purpose — section on page 1.24 : 1, control on section 1.28 : 1 — and the edge and the lit top do the separating, the way they do in the product. Nothing glows, so incoming activity lights the plate instead.

Four more asks, smallest first. All four are now built, and the page above uses all four.

AskWhy Bigwig needs itBuilt as
1 A panel color. One color and an end color for a grouping panel. A section drawn in the controls' gray swallows the buttons on it, and a 1 px edge is the only thing left to tell them apart. It is also what lets a section name clear 4.5: on the lighter plate, orange measures 3.6. PanelFill color, PanelColor, PanelEndColor. A section can also be an outline only.
2 A section name in its own color. It is how the product tells one section from the next. Five of the six slots measure 4.5 or better as a name on a panel. SectionNameInHue, with any of the three header styles.
3 Names inside switches, readable when lit. Switches placed apart from knobs and faders, and a switch lit at full strength is its color top to bottom, with its name in dark ink. Jove asked for placement by kind first. The bottom of a lit plate used to be only half way to its color, and on blue, purple and green neither ink reached 4.5 there. Filled all the way, dark ink measures 5.3 to 10.9 on all six. NamesInsideSwitches. A name is inked against its plate at rest and lit, and a full fill runs top to bottom.
4 A well color for the field of an XY pad, an LFO and a ribbon. The fourth sighting of a recessed style, after Bone's fader slots and Supersaw's ribbon. What you press stands up, and what shows you something is sunk. WellColor, and RecessShadePercent for the shadow inside. A joystick keeps its rings instead.

Two calls that are yours. The lamp ring was your ask when this theme was first drawn. The product's own knobs draw a continuous arc, so this comp does too; the ring stays in the engine and is one setting to put back. And the light cap the product puts on its biggest knobs is shown once, in the second panel, rather than asked for. Left out: the mixer's column fader, a filled column with a bright line instead of a cap, because it needs a slot width and a line-style cap — two properties for one control. Also the filled area under a waveform, power buttons, track color strips and device names. Colors are eyeballed from your screenshots, not sampled from anything of theirs, and every one was then measured.
